YOUR PHOTOS KNOW WHERE YOU LIVE

Here is a small experiment. Open your camera roll, pick any photo you took at home, and run it through a metadata viewer. There is a very good chance you will find your latitude and longitude sitting right there, accurate to a few meters.

I am not exaggerating for effect. Phones write GPS coordinates into photos by default. It lives in a hidden block of the file called EXIF, along with your camera model, the exact second the shot was taken, and sometimes more. You never see it, but it travels with the file everywhere the file goes.

Where this actually bites people

The classic case is selling stuff online. You photograph your old sofa in your living room, upload it to a classifieds site, and the listing now quietly includes the coordinates of your living room. Anyone who downloads the image can read them. Some sites clean this up for you. Plenty do not.

It also happens with photos sent over email, shared through cloud drive links, posted on forums, attached to blog posts, or uploaded to smaller apps that never thought about it. The big social networks mostly strip metadata when you post. Almost nothing else does.

There have been real cases of stalkers finding people through photo metadata, and the famous one where a tech magazine interview revealed a fugitive's location because a staffer posted a photo straight from the phone. This is not a theoretical problem.
